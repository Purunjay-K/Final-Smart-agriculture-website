Sugarcane Crop Stages

From planting to harvest: what happens and how much water the crop needs

Sugarcane grows through four main stages over roughly 10 to 12 months. Each stage has different water needs, so the irrigation advice changes as the crop develops. Timings below are approximate and vary with variety, season, soil and climate.

1. Germination 2. Tillering 3. Grand Growth 4. Maturity

1. Germination

Initial growth stage of the crop.
Duration: about 0 – 45 days Water need: Light but frequent

What happens

  • The buds on the planted sugarcane pieces (setts) sprout and shoots appear above the soil.
  • Roots develop from the base of each sett and begin to take up water.
  • The young shoots are weak, so the plant depends on the food stored in the sett.

Irrigation advice

  • Keep the soil evenly moist, but never waterlogged, because setts can rot in standing water.
  • A light watering soon after planting, followed by regular light irrigation, helps the buds sprout evenly.
  • Dry soil at this stage leads to patchy sprouting and gaps in the field.
Watch for: poor sprouting, gaps in rows and soil crusting. Replant gaps early.

2. Tillering

Stage where new shoots develop.
Duration: about 45 – 120 days Water need: Moderate to high

What happens

  • Each plant produces new side shoots (tillers) from the base, forming a clump.
  • Only the stronger tillers survive and later become millable canes.
  • Leaf area grows fast, and the plant starts to use more water and nutrients.

Irrigation advice

  • Moisture is critical here, because water shortage reduces the number of tillers and so the final yield.
  • Irrigate at regular intervals to keep the root zone moist.
  • Weed control and fertiliser at this stage work best when the soil is moist.
Watch for: yellowing leaves and thin, weak tillers, which usually point to water or nutrient stress.

3. Grand Growth

Rapid growth stage of sugarcane.
Duration: about 120 – 270 days Water need: Highest

What happens

  • The canes elongate quickly, gaining length, thickness and weight.
  • This is the longest stage, and most of the final yield is built during it.
  • The crop's water and nutrient demand is at its peak.

Irrigation advice

  • This is the most water-hungry stage, so do not let the soil dry out.
  • In hot, dry weather, irrigate more often because evaporation is high.
  • Drip irrigation saves water here while keeping moisture steady.
Watch for: leaf curling in the afternoon, short canes and slowed growth. These are signs that the crop needs water.

4. Maturity

Final stage before harvesting.
Duration: about 270 – 360 days Water need: Reduced

What happens

  • Growth slows and the canes store sugar (sucrose) in their stems.
  • Leaves dry and turn yellow, and the canes become hard and ready to cut.
  • Sugar content rises as the crop ripens.

Irrigation advice

  • Cut down on irrigation, because too much water at this stage lowers sugar content.
  • Many growers stop irrigating a few weeks before harvest so the canes can ripen.
  • Avoid waterlogging, which can cause new growth and reduce quality.
Watch for: yellowing top leaves and hard, thick canes. These are signs the crop is ready to harvest.
